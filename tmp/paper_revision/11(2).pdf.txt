ABSTRACT
An analytical dynamic model is proposed to predict the cold start behavior of proton exchange membrane (PEM)
fuel cell. Water phase transition mechanisms have been reconstructed based on the ﬁve states of water migrating
in the fuel cell, composed of water vapor, super-cooled water (liquid water), ice, membrane water (water dis-
solved in solid electrolyte) and frozen membrane water, in order to explore the reasonable water production and
phase transition mechanism. The general water transfer behavior during cold start operation of PEM fuel cell
ﬁnally evolves into four stages: (1) unsaturated water in ionomer and membrane water production; (2) water in
ionomer reaching saturation; (3) over-saturated water in ionomer and quick desorption into liquid and vapor in
pores; and (4) signi ﬁcant ice formation. Both non-equilibrium and equilibrium methods to simulate phase
transition have been carried out to reveal water transport characteristics in the cold start operation. The as-
sumptions associated with the liquid water and vapor production in the electrochemical reaction extensively
involved in the previous modeling studies in the literature should be cautiously used, especially for the dynamic
modeling studies. It is worthwhile addressing that the liquid water inside the cathode CL follows the slow
increasing and rapid decreasing trend during the cold start operation, which indicates that the liquid water
should mostly freeze at the shut-down moment. Furthermore, water prefers to freeze on the interface between
catalyst layer (CL) and micro-porous layer (MPL), leading to the liquid water accumulation here migrating
towards the membrane side.
1. Introduction
Proton exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cell has been attracting
much attention as one of the most promising power plants for portable,
stationary, aerospace and military applications due to its low to zero
emission, ﬂexible size, high energy conversion e ﬃ ciency and low op-
erating noise [1]. For the widespread adoption, enough lifetime and
reliable operation of PEM fuel cell under a wide range of environments
should be realized [2,3]. It is inevitable for PEM fuel cell operating at
subfreezing temperatures. The byproduct water could freeze im-
mediately as the temperature falls down to subfreezing level, which
deﬁnitely brings about reaction kinetics degradation, porous structure
damage and startup failure [4]. Therefore, a solid understanding of cold
start behavior is critical to reinforce the comprehensive operational
reliability of PEM fuel cell [5].
To ﬁgure out the interaction of mass and heat transfer mechanism
and the operational characteristics of PEM fuel cell during cold start,
experimental measurement and modeling simulation is generally

PEM fuel cell, series of startup strategies and optimized cell designs are
further developed. An e ﬃ cient startup scenario was proposed to

the electrode into MPL region for enhancement of cold start perfor-

was put forward by Zhou et al. to activate the dead individual cells in
the cold start operation of fuel cell stacks [18]. Metal foam cathode ﬂow

PEM fuel cell. Bene ﬁt from its extremely porous structure, the resultant
cold startup behavior was signi ﬁcantly strengthened [19]. The me-
chanism of direct catalytic hydrogen-oxygen reaction in anode was
stated by Luo et al. to assist warm up the PEM fuel cell under cold start
operation [20], which also implies that the cross leakage current caused
by the hydrogen crossover through the membrane may be regulated

As aforementioned, the cold startup capability of PEM fuel cell has
been extensively explored in the literature. However, the water pro-
duction and phase transition mechanisms are considered to be im-
mature and need to be further consummated. The super-cooled liquid
water was con ﬁrmed for the ﬁrst time by Ishikawa et al. through a
series of visualization experimental tests on a PEM fuel cell with a
transparency cathode [25]. Super-cooled water was observed on the
surface of gas di ﬀusion layer (GDL) and freeze at the interface between
GDL and CL in cathode [26]. Based on their previous study, the gen-
eration mechanism of super-cooled water was preliminarily built and
theoretically analyzed [27]. Afterwards, limited investigations were
carried out to declare the possible existence of super-cooled water
during the cold start of PEM fuel cell, covering the visualization ex-
perimental tests and theoretical analysis of the behavior of super-cooled
water [9,28,29]. Consequently, the conventional phase transition
system which only contains water vapor, ice and membrane water
(water dissolved in the ionomer) is not rigorous anymore and possibly
unable to accurately predict the water transport inside the PEM fuel
cell. Regarding this issue, the phase transition mechanisms inside the
PEM fuel cell during startup from subfreezing temperature need to be
rebuilt with eager desire.
In this study, dynamic modeling work for the cold start of PEM fuel
cell is presented. Three di ﬀerent water production and phase transition
mechanisms are proposed based on the possible states of byproduct
water, containing water vapor, super-cooled water, ice, non-frozen
membrane water and frozen membrane water. The reasonability of
diﬀerent water transition scenarios formulated in the cold start
behavior, as well as the mass and heat transfer processes are discussed
for the modeling development. Considering the scarcity of the cold start
researches with consideration of super-cooled water, this study quali-
tatively analyzes the important role of super-cooled water playing in
the cold start operation, which should be viewed as an important
supplement to the literature. The transport mechanism of super-cooled
water during the cold start is further investigated and discussed. The
modeling formulation is introduced in the second section. The detailed
resultant modeling prediction, proper discussion and suggestions on
modeling water transfer behavior during cold start operation have been
carried out in the third section, followed by the main conclusions in the
last section.
2. Modeling formulation
2.1. Physical problem
Generally, ﬁve states of water in the cold start operation of PEM fuel
cell should be addressed, composed of water vapor, super-cooled water,
ice, non-frozen membrane water and frozen membrane water. Water
dissolved in the ionomer of CL and membrane is often deﬁ ned as
membrane water (non-frozen or frozen membrane water), while water
behaving in the pore regions of CL, MPL and GDL should be in vapor,
super-cooled, and ice states. Basically, the phase of product water in the
electrochemical reaction is largely dependent on the site of reaction
taking place in cathode CL. Fig. 1 displays the three possible water
states generated in the electrochemical reaction, including membrane
water, water vapor and liquid water. The speci ﬁc morphology of the
incorporation
of carbon supported catalyst (Pt) and ionomer inside CL
should dominate the water generation. Reaction taking place in the
electrolyte will surely generate membrane water, while water produced
in the pores should be in vapor or liquid phase.
In this study, the non-equilibrium water phase transition mechan-
isms are ﬁrstly established in Fig. 2. The di ﬀerent phases of water
produced in the reaction determines the beginning point of the phase
transition, leading to completely di ﬀerent water transfer characteristics
and the resultant cold start behavior. In the following section, the
reasonability of these three water generation mechanisms will be
carefully analyzed and validated for the modeling application in the
literature. Note that in the non-equilibrium phase transition, the

2.2. Assumptions
Several speci ﬁc assumptions have been included:
(1) The ﬂow performed in this model is incompressible and laminar
because of the weak gas pressure gradient and low Reynolds
number.
(2) The gas ﬂow follows the ideal gas law.
(3) The membrane is impermeable to the gas ﬂow.
(4) The e ﬀect of gravity is neglected.
(5) The amount of super-cooled liquid water and vapor is ﬁxed to be
zero in the ﬂow channel due to the fast ﬂow rate.

regularly migrate in the ionomer phase which results in the water

water transfer behavior basically follows the non-equilibrium me-

3.1.2. Equilibrium phase transition model
In this section, the equilibrium phase transition model has been
implemented, as illustrated by the simpli ﬁed approaches for water
generation and phase transition in Fig. 7. For the equilibrium model,
several stages of water transfer behavior inside cathode CL should be
addressed: (1) unsaturated water in ionomer and membrane water
generation; (2) saturated water in ionomer and water vapor generation;
(3) saturated water in ionomer, saturated vapor in pores and liquid
water generation; (4) signi ﬁcant ice formation. It is noteworthy that
both the membrane water and vapor could not get over-saturated in the
equilibrium model. In other words, the equilibrium model does not
explicitly solve the phase transition among the membrane water, liquid
water and vapor. It is assumed that they are instantaneous balanced
during the fuel cell operation.
The experimental comparison has been accordingly updated in
Fig. 8 (a). It is seen that the cell voltage decreases more smoothly and
further approaches the experimental data during the deterioration stage
of the operation compared with the non-equilibrium model. It is
worthwhile addressing that the water transfer behavior should be
consistent with the non-equilibrium method before the membrane
water attains saturated. However, according to the four stages men-
tioned above, liquid water generation is deﬁ nitely accelerated by fur-
ther water production. This could be also construed as the in-
stantaneous membrane water desorption into liquid state, which is
formulated to be liquid water production directly in the equilibrium
model. Obviously, it also slightly decreases the modeling predicted
membrane water content and the output performance on account of the
negative role of liquid water generation playing in the reactant gases
supply.
In detail, equilibrium model exhibits a much consistent behavior
with the experimental data with respect to the voltage decrease, but
only in the initial decrease, and does not accurately predict the shut-
down time. In fact, the equilibrium phase transition cannot be taken as
the real phase transition process. It should be viewed as an simpli ﬁed
equilibrium method for modeling the mass transfer of water. In this
work, the phase change and mass transfer rates are employed to
quantify the water phase transition in the non-equilibrium model. This
makes it available approach the equilibrium state and experimental
data through regulating the water transition processes. Basically, con-
sidering the negligible in ﬂuences of water vapor on the startup opera-
tion, the most important factor comes from the water transfer between
membrane water and liquid water, κ
nfm-lq (s−1). In order to approach
the equilibrium state of water transition, several membrane desorption
(or absorption) rates have been representatively selected as 1, 2 and
3s
−1. The objective of increasing the membrane water desorption rate
is to accelerate membrane water desorption into liquid water and to
promote the phase balance.
Through regulating κ
nfm-lq, the startup performance has been up-
dated based on the non-equilibrium model in Fig. 8(b). More reasonable
evolution of cell output voltage has been achieved. It is evident that the
membrane water desorption rate at around 3 s −1 is deemed to be very
close to the water sorption equilibrium. The variation of membrane
water content is given in Fig. 8(c). Increasing the membrane water
desorption rate directly facilitates water transition from membrane

3.2. Liquid water and vapor generation
On the other hand, as liquid water may be present in the pores of CL
due to the water sorption and desorption from the electrolyte, protons
may ﬂow through the liquid water. It indicates that electrochemical
reaction could also take place in the liquid water cluster. The byproduct
water may be also in liquid state accordingly, as delineated in Fig. 1.
Nevertheless, since the ionic conductivity of proton in liquid phase is
apparently worse than that in the ionomer, membrane water produc-
tion theoretically predominates in the fuel cell operation, particularly in
the early stage of cold starting up. Nonetheless, performance compar-
ison will be still conducted in this section based on three di ﬀerent water
production mechanisms in the electrochemical reaction, e.g. liquid,
vapor and membrane water production, to brie ﬂy examine and point
out their connotation to the numerical results. To brilliantly outline and
compare startup capability, the phase transition rates of water are re-
mained to be 1 s
−1 for all the three cases.
Fig. 9(a) displays the time evolutions of cell voltage based on the
three states of water generation with constant current operation at
0.1 A cm−2 starting up from −20 °C. Initially the averaged cell voltage
in the three cases varies with a similar pace because of the low initial
membrane water content (3.4) and its slow pulling up. For the case of
membrane water generation, a few seconds of delay in liquid and ice
formation is observed due to the unsaturated water in the ionomer. This
manifests that water generated in the ionomer o ﬀers bene ﬁts for pre-
venting water freezing, sustaining better startup capability of PEM fuel
cell, which is consistent with the delay in the induce time of ice for-
mation [25–27]. On the other hand, the results indicate that water
produced in liquid and vapor phase signi ﬁcantly motivates the dete-
rioration of startup capability through directly speeding up ice pre-
cipitation from liquid water. It is also con ﬁrmed by examination of
super-cooled water and ice distribution at 9 s after startup, as per-
formed in Fig. 9(b). The corresponding cell performance deviates from
the experimental results mentioned previously. Moreover, for the liquid
water and vapor generation cases, the modeling predicted liquid and ice
immediately generates from the beginning of the startup operation. It
further illustrates the irrationality of the assumptions associated with
the liquid water and vapor generation not only in the cold start mod-
eling formulation, but also in the general modeling development of
PEM fuel cell.
With regard to the case of membrane water generation, the liquid
water evolution inside the cathode CL follows the slow increasing and
rapid decreasing trend during the cold start operation, which implies
that liquid water should mostly, even completely freeze at the shut-

consideration of super-cooled water. In addition, it can be emphasized
that water vapor production is almost impossible unless the PEM fuel
cell is operating at a high temperature. Liquid water is possibly pro-
duced at a relatively low level in the electrochemical reaction. Hence
this study suggests that the assumptions associated with the liquid
water and vapor production should be cautiously used and even ban-
ished in the numerical works, especially for the dynamic modeling
studies. Proper water transfer rates should be carefully chosen for the
fuel cell operation under various cell design and operational conditions
in order to achieve appropriate water transport behavior and reason-
able modeling predicted results. From a physical standpoint, since the
pore radius inside the electrolyte is always less than the order of 10 nm,
the water ﬂow prefers to be localized rather than connected within the
narrow pores. Hence the water is often recognized to be membrane
water in dissolved state in the ionomer instead of in liquid phase. Since
the catalyst is usually assembled through spraying the catalyst-ionomer
ink onto the polymer membrane, it reveals that the active area for the
electrochemical reaction on the surface of catalyst particle should be
mostly wrapped in the electrolyte, as shown in Fig. 1. As is well-known,
the ionic group of sulfonic acid (SO
3
−H+) will rarely retain less than
three water molecules per proton. Subsequently, once a water molecule
is formed at the triple-phase zone, it participates in the water molecules
group connected with the sulfonic acid group. This further indicates
that the membrane water production should play the leading role in the
water generation mechanism in the electrochemical reaction.
4. Conclusions
In this study, an analytical multiphase model for the PEM fuel cell
has been proposed to investigate the self-startup behavior from sub-
freezing temperature. In consideration of the exiguity of the researches
on the super-cooled water transfer characteristics in the PEM fuel cell at
subfreezing temperature, the super-cooled water transport has been
performed to enrich the water transfer mechanism in PEM fuel cell. In
order to explore the reasonable water transfer behavior, water phase
transition mechanisms have been rebuilt based on the states of water
generation in cathode CL, including water vapor, membrane water and
super-cooled water. The general water transfer behavior during cold
start ﬁnally evolves into four stages: (1) membrane water generation
and unsaturated water in ionomer; (2) water in ionomer reaching sa-
turation; (3) over-saturated water in ionomer and quick desorption into
liquid and vapor in pores; and (4) signi ﬁcant ice formation. The un-
reliable assumption associated with the vapor and liquid production in
cathode reaction should be cautiously used, even prevented from the
numerical model, especially for the dynamic modeling formulation.
The results indicate that the startup capability bene ﬁts a lot from
water generation in membrane water state, which greatly helps delay
the water freezing, and signi ﬁcantly slows down the ice growth inside
the cathode CL. It is worthwhile addressing that the liquid water inside
the cathode CL follows the slow increasing and rapid decreasing trend
during the cold start operation, which indicates that liquid water should
mostly, even completely freeze at the shut-down moment. On the other
hand, liquid water accumulation gradually migrates from the interface
of CL and MPL to the center of CL, impeded by the severe ice blockage
and guided by the variation of reaction kinetics. Furthermore, the water
transition associated with vapor is observed much weaker than any
other water phase transitions owing to the intrinsic small amount of
vapor in the pores. This study is aiming to present basic framework to
possibly optimize the water balance inside the PEM fuel cell during cold
start operation by reasonably regulating water generation, phase tran-
sition and transport. It is expected that this study could o ﬀer useful
insights into the cold start enhancement of PEM fuel cell, as well as the
reasonable numerical modeling formulations in the literature.
